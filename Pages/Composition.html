Composition

A single pixel is capable of surrounding itself with specifically selected pixels at each surrounding position.
This is for one reason:
At any given time, There may only be one pixel of SPWN / SPWN2

To rephrase this in a way that is easier to understand, executing particles in this order:
PCLN(PHOT) [life = 10]
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
CONV(LIGH) [tmp = PHOT]

Will accomplish the following:
Particle is surrounded with PHOT.
First PHOT detected (top left) is CONVerted to SPWN, the rest are not.
The one SPWN is CONVerted to an example element, in this case, IRON.
Remaining PHOT is converted to LIGH as cleanup.

What we have just accomplished, to state it a third time, is making a particle specifically in the top left position.
And it doesnt stop there, of course.
What if we continue?
What if we were to...
PCLN(PHOT) [life = 10]
----0
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
----1, Top Left
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
-CONV(VIBR) [tmp = SPWN]-
----2, Middle Left
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
----3, Bottom Right
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
----4, Top Middle
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
----5, Bottom Middle
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
CONV(IRON) [tmp = SPWN]
----6, Top Right
CONV(SPWN) [tmp = PHOT]
-CONV(RSSS) [tmp = SPWN]-
----7, Middle Right
CONV(LIGH) [tmp = IRON]
CONV(LIGH) [tmp = PHOT]


With that, we have placed VIBR on the middle left, and RSSS on the middle right, then wiped the remainder away.
Surely you can imagine what we can do with that, right?
